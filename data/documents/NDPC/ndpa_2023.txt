NIGERIA DATA PROTECTION ACT 2023
Federal Republic of Nigeria
Signed into Law: June 14, 2023

PART I — ESTABLISHMENT AND OBJECTIVES

Section 1. Establishment of the Nigeria Data Protection Commission
1.1 There is established the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (the Commission).
1.2 The Commission shall be a body corporate with perpetual succession and
a common seal, and may sue and be sued in its corporate name.

Section 2. Objectives of the Act
The objectives of this Act are to:
(a) regulate the processing of personal data of data subjects in Nigeria;
(b) ensure that the right to privacy of natural persons is protected;
(c) foster safe conduct for transactions involving the exchange of personal data;
(d) prevent manipulation of personal data.

PART II — DATA PROTECTION PRINCIPLES

Section 3. Lawful Processing of Personal Data
3.1 Personal data shall be processed only if at least one of the following
conditions is met:
(a) the data subject has given consent to the processing;
(b) processing is necessary for the performance of a contract;
(c) processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation;
(d) processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject.

Section 4. Rights of Data Subjects
4.1 Every data subject has the right to:
(a) be informed of the processing of their personal data;
(b) access their personal data held by a data controller;
(c) request rectification of inaccurate personal data;
(d) request erasure of personal data where processing is unlawful;
(e) object to processing of their personal data.

Section 5. Data Retention
5.1 Personal data shall not be retained longer than is necessary for the
purpose for which it was collected.
5.2 Notwithstanding subsection 5.1, data may be retained longer where
required by any applicable law or regulation.
5.3 A data controller that retains personal data beyond the permitted
period is liable to a fine of not less than N2,000,000.

PART III — OBLIGATIONS OF DATA CONTROLLERS

Section 6. Registration Requirements
6.1 Every data controller of major importance shall register with the Commission.
6.2 A data controller that processes the personal data of more than 2,000
data subjects within a 12-month period shall be deemed of major importance.
6.3 Registration shall be renewed annually and failure to renew attracts
a penalty of N2,000,000 per month of default.

Section 7. Data Protection Officer
7.1 Every data controller of major importance shall designate a Data
Protection Officer (DPO).
7.2 The DPO shall be responsible for ensuring compliance with this Act
and shall report directly to the highest level of management.
7.3 The DPO shall be accessible to data subjects at all times.

Section 8. Penalties for Non-Compliance
8.1 A data controller that fails to comply with this Act is liable to:
(a) in the case of an individual, a fine of not less than N2,000,000;
(b) in the case of a company, a fine of 2% of annual gross revenue
    or N10,000,000, whichever is greater.
8.2 Repeated violations may attract a fine of up to 10% of annual
gross revenue.
8.3 The Commission may in addition to fines, order the suspension
of data processing activities.

PART IV — CROSS-BORDER DATA TRANSFERS

Section 9. Transfer of Personal Data Outside Nigeria
9.1 A data controller shall not transfer personal data to a foreign
country unless that country provides an adequate level of protection.
9.2 Adequacy shall be determined by the Commission based on:
(a) the rule of law and human rights record of the receiving country;
(b) the existence of a supervisory authority in that country;
(c) international commitments entered into by that country.
9.3 Where adequacy cannot be established, transfer may proceed only
with the explicit consent of the data subject.
