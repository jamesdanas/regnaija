NIGERIA TAX ACT 2025 (NTA)
Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)
Signed into Law: June 26, 2025
Effective Date: January 1, 2026

PART I — IMPOSITION OF TAX

Section 1. Charge of Tax
1.1 Tax is hereby imposed on income, transactions and instruments
as provided in this Act.
1.2 This Act repeals and consolidates the following legislation:
(a) Companies Income Tax Act (CITA);
(b) Personal Income Tax Act (PITA);
(c) Petroleum Profits Tax Act (PPTA);
(d) Value Added Tax Act (VAT);
(e) Capital Gains Tax Act (CGTA);
(f) Stamp Duties Act (SDA).

Section 2. Classification of Companies
2.1 For the purposes of this Act, companies are classified as:
(a) Small companies — annual gross turnover of N50,000,000 or less
    with total fixed assets not exceeding N250,000,000;
(b) Large companies — all other companies not qualifying as small.
2.2 Provided that any business providing professional services shall
not be classified as a small company regardless of turnover.

Section 3. Rate of Tax
3.1 Small companies shall be exempt from Companies Income Tax at a
rate of 0% on their assessable profits.
3.2 Large companies shall be taxed at a flat rate of 30% of
assessable profits.
3.3 The previous three-tier tax structure under the repealed CITA
is hereby abolished with effect from January 1, 2026.

Section 4. Development Levy
4.1 A Development Levy of 4% on assessable profits shall apply to
all companies except small companies.
4.2 This levy consolidates and replaces the following:
(a) Tertiary Education Tax of 3%;
(b) NITDA Levy of 1%;
(c) NASENI Levy of 0.25%;
(d) Police Trust Fund Levy of 0.005%.

Section 5. Capital Gains Tax
5.1 The Capital Gains Tax rate for companies is hereby increased
from 10% to 30%, aligning with the Companies Income Tax rate.
5.2 CGT now applies to disposals of digital and virtual assets
including cryptocurrencies and NFTs.
5.3 Provided that primary residence disposals remain exempt from CGT.

Section 6. Digital and Virtual Assets
6.1 Banks and Virtual Asset Service Providers must report transactions
exceeding N25,000,000 for individuals and N100,000,000 for companies.
6.2 All Virtual Asset Service Providers must register with the NRS
within 90 days of commencement of this Act.
6.3 Withholding tax deducted shall be remitted to the NRS within
30 days of deduction.
6.4 Failure to remit attracts a penalty of 10% of the unremitted
amount plus interest at the prevailing CBN benchmark lending rate.

PART II — FILING AND COMPLIANCE

Section 7. Filing Requirements
7.1 Every company shall file its self-assessment tax return not later
than six months after the end of its accounting year.
7.2 Failure to file within the stipulated period shall attract a
penalty of N25,000 for the first month of default and N5,000 for
each subsequent month.
7.3 The NRS may extend filing deadlines in cases of natural disaster
or other exceptional circumstances upon written application.

Section 8. Enforcement Powers of the NRS
8.1 The NRS is empowered to inspect business premises and appoint
third-party collection agents for unpaid taxes.
8.2 The NRS may seize assets where taxes remain unpaid after
a final demand notice of 30 days.
8.3 Taxpayers retain rights to advance rulings on transactions
and may apply for negotiated settlements.

PART III — MULTINATIONAL COMPANIES

Section 9. Minimum Effective Tax Rate
9.1 Multinational corporations with global revenues of EUR 750 million
or Nigerian annual turnover above N50 billion must pay a minimum
15% effective tax rate on net income earned in Nigeria.
9.2 Where the effective tax rate paid falls below 15%, a top-up
tax shall be assessed and collected by the NRS.
9.3 This provision aligns Nigeria with the OECD Pillar Two framework
on global minimum taxation.
